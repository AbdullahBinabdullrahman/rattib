# Rattib — Market Research & Validation

Saudi experiences marketplace (Airbnb-style). Research current as of August 2026.

---

## 1. Verdict up front

**The market is real, large, and growing — but it is a *domestic* market, not an inbound-tourist market, and the licensing regime is the single thing most likely to kill a naive "anyone can host" model.**

| Question | Answer | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is there enough demand? | Yes — 93.3M domestic tourists, SAR 127.1B domestic spend in 2025 | High |
| Is there enough supply? | Yes, but it is informal (Instagram/WhatsApp) and needs to be formalised | Medium |
| Is the space already taken? | Partly. Adjacent players exist; the *self-serve long-tail marketplace* slot is open | Medium |
| Can an individual legally host? | Yes, with a **freelance certificate** (free, instant) plus a category-specific activity licence. Still the #1 risk area | High |
| Is women-only a real segment? | Yes — and it is a supply-side unlock, not just a filter | High |

## 2. Market size and direction

### 2025 — record year
- **123M total tourists** (domestic + inbound), ~+6% YoY.
- **SAR 304B (~$81B)** total tourism spend — an all-time record.
- **Domestic: 93.3M tourists, SAR 127.1B spend**, domestic tourist count **+8.3% YoY**, overnight domestic trips +4.7%.
- Inbound: 29.3M tourists, SAR 176.6B spend.
- Tourism ≈ **4.9% of GDP** (2024 basis), growing 14% YoY; ~**1.03M** tourism-sector employees.

### 2026 — the important divergence
- **Inbound fell 13% in Q1 2026** to 8.3M, driven by regional conflict (the whole Middle East was −14% against +2% globally). Saudi outperformed the region but still contracted. Officials describe 5–6% decline over the first five months as acceptable under conditions.
- **Domestic absorbed the shock**: domestic was **78% of all visitors** in Q1 2026, with trips **+16% to ~29M**. Q1 2026 tourism spend still hit ~$22B.

### What this means for us
> Build domestic-first. A platform whose demand depends on international arrivals would have lost 13% of its market in a single quarter through no fault of its own. The Saudi resident booking a Thursday-evening pottery session in Riyadh is the resilient customer.

Inbound is upside, not the base case. It changes the roadmap: Arabic-first UI, mada/STC Pay/Tabby before international cards, weekend and same-day booking patterns over multi-day tour itineraries.

## 3. Competitive landscape

### Tier 1 — direct, and very recent

**flynas × Saudi Tourism Authority — "Saudi Experiences"** (launched **July 2026**)
- 150+ curated cultural, lifestyle and adventure activities across 15 destinations (Riyadh, Jeddah, AlUla, Asir…).
- Explicitly includes **traditional pottery and cooking classes** — the same categories in our concept.
- 10 languages, real-time availability, instant confirmation, localised payment options.
- Distributed through the flynas website/app — i.e. an **airline attach-rate play** aimed at travellers who already bought a flight.

**Read:** this is the closest thing to us and it launched a month ago. But it is a *curated catalogue* (~150 SKUs, hand-picked, partner-supplied), not a self-serve two-sided marketplace. There is no host portal, no long tail, no map-first spontaneous discovery, no gender policy primitive. It validates the category and takes the "premium curated inbound" position. It does not take the "500 hosts in Riyadh, book something tonight" position.

**webook.com** (formerly HalaYalla; Saudi entertainment/sports tech, PIF-adjacent ecosystem)
- The incumbent super app for **tickets and events**: sports, concerts, entertainment, and now expanding into flights, hotels, restaurant reservations, sports facilities and live streaming.
- Targeting a **billion-dollar valuation and an eventual listing**; acquired Portugal-based SmartMove for a European base.
- Context: GEA reported **89M+ visitors to entertainment activities in 2025**.

**Read:** enormous distribution moat and the strongest brand for "things to do in Saudi." Weak where we are strong: it is built for **high-volume ticketed events**, not 8-seat intimate workshops with a named human host, host profiles, ratings, and request-to-book. If webook decides to build the long tail, they are the existential competitor — speed and host loyalty are our defence.

### Tier 2 — adjacent
- **Saudi Tourism Authority unified platforms** (e.g. the Saudi Summer 2026 platform, 50+ private-sector partners) — seasonal campaign aggregators, not year-round marketplaces. More likely a **distribution partner than a competitor**.
- **Airbnb Experiences** — relaunched 2025 with Services and Originals after a two-year freeze; global brand and playbook, thin Saudi supply, no Arabic-first product, no gender primitives.
- **GetYourGuide / Viator (Tripadvisor) / Klook** — strong inbound OTAs, supply skewed to established licensed tour operators, not individual artisans.
- **Platinumlist, Almosafer** — ticketing/OTA adjacency.
- **Hido** — small local Saudi tourism app built around local guides. Direct in spirit, small in scale.

### Tier 3 — the actual incumbent
**Instagram DMs + WhatsApp groups + bank transfer.**
This is where the pottery studio, the home baker, the coffee roaster and the nabati poetry majlis actually sell today. No availability management, no payment protection, no discovery beyond followers, no reviews, constant no-shows.

> Our real competitor is not a company, it is a workflow. That is the best kind of competitor to have — it means we are not fighting for share, we are formalising a market. It also sets the bar: we must be *better than a DM*, which means near-zero-friction listing and instant payouts, not enterprise tour-operator software.

## 4. Supply-side validation

There is a **pre-qualified, government-credentialed pool of hosts** already assembled:

- **2025 was declared the "Year of Handicrafts"** by the Ministry of Culture / Heritage Commission.
- The Heritage Commission has **licensed 4,800+ artisans through the Abde'a platform** (منصة أبدع, the Ministry of Culture's cultural-licences portal at `abdea.moc.gov.sa`) in 2025 alone. Its **Crafts Practitioner licence** (رخصة الحرفي) explicitly permits citizens to work in and sell handmade craft fields.
- Programmes exist to train and commercialise them: **Artisan's House** training courses, and **Banan** (Saudi International Handicrafts Week) in Riyadh.
- Stated policy goal: *create sustainable income opportunities for artisans and integrate handicrafts into the creative economy.* That is, almost verbatim, our value proposition.

**Actionable:** an Abde'a Crafts Practitioner licence is a ready-made **verification credential** for craft hosts, and the Heritage Commission's artisan roster is a warm supply-acquisition list. Categories that map directly: pottery, sadu weaving, khoos (palm frond), bakery, coffee roasting, calligraphy, incense/oud blending — and the five crafts the Kingdom counts as its main ones (hand embroidery, al-Sadu, aromatic plant products, traditional sweets, palm frond products) account for about a third of all artisans.

Second, and easier to miss: the **freelance certificate** (وثيقة العمل الحر, MHRSD, `freelance.sa`) is **free, instant, requires no commercial registration**, spans **160+ approved activities**, and is organised into eight programmes — four of which are our host persona pre-packaged by the government: **handicrafts, productive families, rural development, and specialized services**. It also grants a dedicated commercial bank account, which is what we need for payouts. This is the single lowest-friction path from "Instagram home baker" to "legally transacting host," and onboarding should walk hosts through it.

Also relevant: the **Tourism Development Fund (TDF)** runs tourism startup programmes with digital lending and AWN loans for small operators and tour operators — a financing path for hosts scaling up, and a potential partnership channel.

## 5. The female segment — stronger than expected

- **44% of travellers to Saudi Arabia in 2024 were women** — over **13 million women**.
- Saudi Arabia is ranked the **safest G20 country for solo female travellers**.
- **45% of the Saudi tourism workforce is female**; the Vice Minister of Tourism is HH Princess Haifa bint Mohammed Al Saud.
- Women-only trips and packages are an explicitly growing product category, alongside heritage tourism and wellness retreats.

**The non-obvious insight:** gender-locked experiences are usually framed as a *demand-side filter* ("let women find women-only classes"). The bigger effect is **supply-side**. A large population of skilled women — home bakers, weavers, henna artists, perfumers — will host **only** if they can guarantee a women-only room. Without that guarantee they never list at all. Gender lock is therefore a **supply unlock**, and supply is the hard side of any marketplace.

This alone is a credible differentiator against flynas/STA, Airbnb, GetYourGuide and Klook, none of which model audience policy as a first-class object.

## 6. Regulatory reality — read this before writing code

### 6.1 Host licensing (the big one)
A Ministry of Tourism licence is legally required for anyone offering tour operations, guided tours, **experience-based tourism**, and organised excursions. Current MT application requirements include:

- a valid **Commercial Registration** with the correct tourism activity,
- **Chamber of Commerce** membership,
- an official **office lease inside Saudi Arabia**,
- a registered **National Address**,
- an active **local bank account**,
- accounts on the required government platforms.

**Implication: the pure Airbnb model — an individual signs up in five minutes and sells — does not transfer cleanly.** This must be designed around, not discovered in month six.

But the picture is less bleak than that requirement list alone suggests, because **not every experience is a "tourism activity."** Two facts reframe it:

- **Economic identity is cheap.** An individual does not need a CR to transact legally. The **freelance certificate** (وثيقة العمل الحر, MHRSD via `freelance.sa`) is free, instant, requires no commercial registration, lets the holder operate under their personal name, opens a commercial bank account, and is an accepted basis for regular e-commerce activity. Its handicrafts / productive-families / rural-development programmes fit our hosts directly.
- **Activity licensing is category-specific.** The MT regime bites hardest on **guiding, excursions, transport and accommodation**. A paid pottery or cooking workshop sold to residents sits much closer to a craft or training service, and the Ministry of Culture's **Abde'a Crafts Practitioner licence** already covers practising and selling in handmade craft fields. Culinary work pulls in municipal (Balady) food-handling and premises permits instead.

So eligibility is **three layers, not one**: (1) economic identity — CR or freelance certificate, always; (2) an activity licence appropriate to the category; (3) our own trust layer (Nafath, QA, insurance, ratings). See product brief §2.2 for the full model.

One firm constraint for the tourism-regulated categories: the individual **tourist-guide licence** exists (Saudi national, 18+, medically fit, accredited training and assessment, General Guide vs. Area Guide) — but licensed guides operate **through registered travel and tourism service providers**. Individuals can be licensed; they cannot be the merchant of record. Note also that tourism roles such as reception, booking and sales carry **high Saudization ratios, some 70%+** — a hiring constraint on us as an operator, not on hosts.

Mitigation paths, in order of preference:

1. **Marketplace-of-record for regulated categories.** Rattib holds the tourism licence and contracts experts under it. Highest compliance burden on us, lowest friction for hosts, fastest supply growth — and it is the structure the guide-licence rule already implies. Probably right for v1.
2. **Freelance certificate as the supply floor.** Require it of every host, default to it over CR, and link hosts to `freelance.sa` during onboarding. CR becomes an upgrade path for hosts who hire, take partners, or approach the VAT threshold.
3. **Credential engine.** `host_credentials` as a first-class entity — CR, freelance certificate, Abde'a craft licence, municipal craft licence, food/health permit, MT licence, guide licence — with issuing body, expiry tracking, verification state, and a category → required-credentials matrix held as configuration. Gate at publish time, auto-pause on expiry. Necessary under every path above.
4. **Category sequencing.** Launch crafts and culinary in already-suitable premises; defer guided outdoor tours, desert excursions and anything involving transport or wilderness. The legal, risk and commercial arguments all point the same way.
5. **Partner-fronted supply.** Onboard licensed operators who bring several experts under their licence.

### 6.2 Payments
- Governed by SAMA's **Law of Payments and Payment Services** and its Implementing Regulations. Collecting money from customers and paying out to hosts is a **split-payout / escrow-flavoured model**, and PSPs must **segregate or escrow customer funds**.
- Pure linkage and technical support for e-commerce payments does not currently require a SAMA licence, but requires technical permits from **Saudi Payments**.
- **Do not build a wallet or hold funds ourselves.** Use a licensed PSP with marketplace split payouts. Support **mada, Apple Pay, STC Pay, and Tabby** (BNPL matters for higher-ticket experiences) before international cards.
- Treat the exact regulatory model as an explicit discovery item with counsel before launch.

### 6.3 Other
- **ZATCA e-invoicing (Fatoora) phase 2** — compliant invoices for both sides, 15% VAT treatment on the commission and on the experience.
- **PDPL** — personal data protection. Relevant to the gender feature: gender is personal data, must be minimised, and other guests' identities must never be exposed to each other.

## 7. Positioning

**What we are:** the Arabic-first, domestic-first, map-first marketplace where any verified Saudi expert can publish a small-group experience today and fill the seats tonight — with audience policy (women-only, families, mixed) as a native primitive.

**What we are not:** an inbound OTA, an events ticketing platform, or a curated 150-SKU catalogue.

| Dimension | flynas/STA | webook | Airbnb/GYG | Instagram | **Rattib** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Self-serve host onboarding | ✗ | partial | ✓ | ✓ | **✓** |
| Long-tail supply | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | **✓** |
| Domestic-first | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | **✓** |
| Map-first discovery | ✗ | ✗ | partial | ✗ | **✓** |
| Same-day / tonight | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | **✓** |
| Gender-locked audience | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | manual | **✓** |
| Payment protection & reviews | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | **✓** |

## 8. Risks, honestly

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Host licensing blocks individual supply | **High** (was critical — freelance certificate de-risks the identity layer) | Marketplace-of-record; freelance certificate as supply floor; category sequencing; credential engine (§6.1) |
| Credential requirement adds onboarding friction | Medium | Gate at publish not signup; link hosts straight to `freelance.sa`; concierge the first cohort |
| webook builds the long tail | High | Speed; host exclusivity via tooling and payouts, not contracts |
| Cold-start liquidity | High | One city, one vertical cluster, concierge onboarding |
| Hosts disintermediate after first booking | High | Own rebooking, reviews, payment protection; make repeat cheaper on-platform |
| flynas/STA locks up premium supply | Medium | We want the long tail they cannot serve; explore being their supply layer |
| Inbound-dependent forecasts | Medium | Already de-risked by going domestic-first |
| Gender enforcement fails (mis-declared identity) | Medium | Nafath-verified identity for gender-locked bookings (§ product brief) |
| Payments/escrow misclassification | Medium | Licensed PSP, counsel review pre-launch |

## 9. What to validate next (before building the full platform)

Cheap experiments, in order:

1. **Licensing feasibility** — one meeting with MT and a tourism-licensing consultant, with two specific questions: *(a) is a paid craft or cooking workshop for residents a regulated tourism activity, or a craft/training service?* and *(b) can an individual expert holding a freelance certificate transact under our licence?* The launch category scope depends on (a); the whole structure depends on (b). **Blocking.**
2. **Supply density, one city** — can we source **30 hosts in Riyadh** across crafts + culinary + culture in four weeks? If sourcing 30 is hard, sourcing 3,000 is impossible.
3. **Women-only supply lift** — of those 30, how many say they would host *only* if women-only were guaranteed? If the number is material (>25%), the feature is a moat, not a checkbox.
4. **Willingness to pay** — landing page + WhatsApp concierge booking for 20 real sessions. Measure fill rate and no-show rate before writing a booking engine.
5. **Take-rate tolerance** — what commission do hosts accept before threatening to go back to DMs? Benchmark: global experience platforms sit around 20%; informal Saudi hosts currently pay 0%.

**Kill criteria:** if (1) says individuals cannot transact under any structure, and (2) yields fewer than 10 willing hosts, the Airbnb-style model is wrong and the business is a licensed operator with a booking site instead.
